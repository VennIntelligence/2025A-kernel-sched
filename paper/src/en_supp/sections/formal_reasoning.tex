\section{Formal Results and Their Boundaries}\label{sec:supp-formal}

This section gives the complete claims supported by the current evidence. The proofs deliberately distinguish an accounting identity, a capacity-relaxation lower bound, a fixed-order certificate, and global optimality over topological orders; none may be substituted for another.

\subsection{Canonical traffic decomposition and the factor-two bound}

For residency interruption $i$, let $v_i$ be the buffer size, and let $d_i=v_i$ if the buffer is unbacked and $d_i=0$ otherwise. Define $V=\sum_i v_i$ and $D=\sum_i d_i$.

\paragraph{Proposition 1 (accounting identity and tight bounds).}
Every legal P2/P3 artifact satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:supp-identity-proof}
 E=V+D,\qquad V\le E\le 2V.
\end{equation}
At fixed $V>0$, changing only the class composition of spilled bytes can change the traffic ratio between two artifacts by at most two, and this bound is attainable.

\paragraph{Proof.}
A backed interruption costs $v_i$, while an unbacked interruption costs $2v_i=v_i+d_i$. Summing term by term gives $E=\sum_i(v_i+d_i)=V+D$. Since $d_i\in\{0,v_i\}$, $0\le D\le V$, hence $V\le E\le2V$. If all spilled bytes are backed, $D=0,E=V$; if all are unbacked, $D=V,E=2V$. Both endpoints are attainable.\hfill$\square$

Proposition 1 is an evaluator accounting property, not a causal mechanism claim. It neither proves that production candidate-order generation reads class labels (it does not) nor that $D$ is more important than $V$. A mechanism comparison must report both $\Delta V$ and $\Delta D$; at fixed $V$, class composition alone cannot produce a gain greater than two.

\subsection{Fixed-order residency-gap model}

Fix a legal original topological order $\pi$. For each buffer $b$, sort its mandatory-event positions as $p_{b,0}<p_{b,1}<\cdots<p_{b,r_b}$. The open interval between consecutive events defines a residency gap $g=(b,j)$. Binary variable $x_g=1$ keeps $b$ continuously resident from event $j$ to event $j+1$; $x_g=0$ permits an interruption. Let
\begin{equation}
 w_g=\begin{cases}
 s_b,&b\text{ is input-backed},\\
 2s_b,&b\text{ is unbacked}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}

For memory type $k$ and schedule cut $t$, let $P_{k,t}$ be the bytes mandatorily pinned at that cut, let $\mathcal G_{k,t}$ be the optional same-type gaps crossing it, and let capacity be $B_k$. The fixed-order weighted-gap model is
\begin{align}
 L^\star=\min_x\quad &\sum_g w_g(1-x_g),\label{eq:supp-gap-obj}\\
 \text{s.t.}\quad &P_{k,t}+\sum_{g\in\mathcal G_{k,t}}s_gx_g\le B_k,
 &&\forall k,t,\label{eq:supp-gap-cap}\\
 &x_g\in\{0,1\}.\nonumber
\end{align}
If $P_{k,t}>B_k$ at any cut, spilling cannot repair the fixed order because the mandatory pinned set of one event already exceeds capacity.

\paragraph{Proposition 2 (fixed-order traffic lower bound).}
The optimum $L^\star$ of Eqs.~\eqref{eq:supp-gap-obj}--\eqref{eq:supp-gap-cap} is no greater than the traffic of any legal fixed-order plan with contiguous physical addresses.

\paragraph{Proof.}
Take any legal physical plan $A$. Set $x_g=1$ exactly when the buffer remains continuously resident throughout gap $g$, and set it to zero otherwise. At every cut, the mandatory residency segments and every selected continuous gap really do occupy the same memory type simultaneously. Because $A$ is legal, their total size is at most $B_k$, so the constructed $x$ satisfies every capacity constraint.

Every gap with $x_g=0$ contains at least one complete residency interruption, so $A$ pays at least $w_g$ there; an artificial plan could interrupt the same gap more than once and pay more. Thus $E(A)\ge\sum_gw_g(1-x_g)\ge L^\star$. The argument never uses the concrete addresses of $A$, so it holds for every legal contiguous-address plan.\hfill$\square$

\subsection{From a lower bound to a machine-checkable certificate}

The capacity relaxation discards address-shape information, so its solution need not be packable even when all addresses may be globally reassigned from scratch. Consider a discrete storage-allocation counterexample with capacity four. Seven segments have ``active cuts/size''
\[
 A:\{0\}/2,\ B:\{0,1\}/2,\ C:\{1,2\}/1,\
 D:\{1,2,3\}/1,\ E:\{2,3\}/1,\
 F:\{3,4\}/2,\ G:\{4\}/2.
\]
The active volumes at the five cuts are $4,4,3,4,4$, so every cumulative-capacity constraint holds, yet no set of fixed contiguous offsets exists. At cut 0, $A$ and $B$ must occupy the two length-two halves; without loss of generality take $B=[0,2)$. At cut 1, $C$ and $D$ must occupy $[2,3)$ and $[3,4)$. If $D=[2,3)$, then $E$ at cut 2 must occupy a unit position in $[0,2)$; at cut 3, $D$ and $E$ fragment the remaining space so the length-two $F$ cannot fit. If $D=[3,4)$, only $E=[0,1)$ permits $F=[1,3)$ at cut 3; then at cut 4 only one unit remains on each side of $F$, so length-two $G$ cannot fit. Other $E$ positions already fail at cut 3, and the left--right mirror is symmetric. The gap model accepts these intervals because it sees only volume; ``gap optimal'' alone therefore proves neither physical feasibility nor canonical validity.

\paragraph{Theorem 3 (fixed-order traffic certificate).}
For a given fixed order, suppose that
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the gap model proves optimum $L^\star$;
  \item the selected gaps are realized as contiguous-address residency segments and every spill-out/spill-in dependency is legal; and
  \item the canonical evaluator returns zero violations and computes $E(A)=L^\star$.
\end{enumerate}
Then artifact $A$ has minimum extra traffic for that fixed order.

\paragraph{Proof.}
By Proposition 2, every legal fixed-order physical plan $A'$ satisfies $E(A')\ge L^\star$. Conditions 2--3 make $A$ a legal plan with $E(A)=L^\star$, so no legal plan under the same order has lower traffic.\hfill$\square$

The qualifiers in Theorem 3 are essential. ``OPTIMAL'' from greedy packing or \texttt{NoOverlap2D} means that its satisfaction model found or proved a layout; it is not a second layout-quality optimization. Packing failure or timeout does not invalidate $L^\star$ as a lower bound. The certificate also does not minimize spill count or time among traffic ties, so it is not complete P2 optimality; fixed-order traffic optimality is not global optimality over topological orders.

\subsection{Candidate-portfolio monotonicity}

Let $\mathcal A$ be the artifacts successfully generated inside the candidate loop, and let $K$ be the implementation's P2- or P3-semantic lexicographic key. The production solver returns $A^\star=\arg\min_{A\in\mathcal A}K(A)$.

\paragraph{Proposition 4 (internal-key monotonicity under candidate expansion).}
For any additional successfully generated candidate set $\mathcal B$,
\begin{equation}
 \min_{A\in\mathcal A\cup\mathcal B}K(A)
 \ \le_{\mathrm{lex}}\
 \min_{A\in\mathcal A}K(A).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Proof.}
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal A\cup\mathcal B$, so the previous best candidate remains in the expanded set; minimizing over the larger set cannot be worse.\hfill$\square$

This is monotonicity of the portfolio's \emph{internal selection key}, not a causal claim about a new candidate or a legality theorem. Calling the return value a legal artifact additionally requires a correct generator or canonical evaluation of the returned artifact. The production loop does not invoke that evaluator on every candidate; our experiments report only final artifacts with zero violations. If the portfolio contains a strong baseline order, non-regression against it follows from set inclusion and does not attribute an improvement to ordering, placement, or a victim component. Production selection computes the actual $K_2/K_3$; it needs no overflow-area surrogate theorem.

\subsection{Complexity and exact-backend boundary}

For fixed numbers of candidate orders, victim policies, and early-reload windows, the scalable path performs topological ordering, residency scans, address placement, and timing simulation. A placement request may scan the resident set or free intervals, giving a conservative quadratic term in the number of buffers; the portfolio contributes only a fixed multiplier. This is an implementation upper bound, not a linear-time guarantee.

Weighted-gap selection and fallback \texttt{NoOverlap2D} are exponential in the worst case. The CP-SAT backend is therefore an offline research oracle, not part of canonical \texttt{solve}. A future integration would need a size threshold or timeout and a validated fallback when optimization is unfinished, packing fails, or validation fails. Nonzero gaps, timeouts, and not-run cases in the current experiments must be reported as such and cannot be extrapolated into universal exactness.
