\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This work recasts NPU kernel memory planning as two separable questions: which
legal order exposes a tractable residency frontier, and which backed or
generated gaps should remain resident under capacity.  Our production answer is
a bounded, exact-objective portfolio built around dependency-frontier ordering;
our fixed-order research answer is a weighted-gap lower bound coupled to a
contiguous-packing traffic certificate.  The identity $\mathrm{Tr}=\mathrm{Vol}+\mathrm{Dt}$ keeps the source
of every P2 change auditable throughout.

The evidence is useful but deliberately narrow.  The scalable solver improves
five of six public P2 cases and ties the sixth, and improves P3 time on five of
six.  Nonuniform repair studies help the two convolution cases, and the
canonically validated synthetic re-evaluation establishes non-regression and
small-scale same-order traffic optimality rather than new generalization.  The result is therefore not a universal clean/dirty scheduling
principle; it is an honest exact-to-heuristic bridge together with a validated
improvement on the studied kernel family.  Future work should integrate guarded
exact solving behind a timeout with a validated fallback, derive a cheaper and
uniform cost-aware order repair, and evaluate on broader hardware traces whose
read/write semantics are richer than the current static benchmark labels.
