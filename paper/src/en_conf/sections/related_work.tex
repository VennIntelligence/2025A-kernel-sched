\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Joint scheduling and memory optimization has a long history in DNN accelerator
compilation. COSMA~\citep{li2023combined}, for example, observes that
non-compulsory off-chip accesses depend jointly on operator schedule, memory
allocation, and tensor replacement, and optimizes the three with an ILP. That
line of work solves a graph-level placement and replacement problem. In
contrast, NPU intra-kernel scratchpad scheduling operates at a much finer
micro-operation granularity: clean and dirty buffer states introduce a
$2\times$ spill-cost asymmetry, and schedule order determines not only the live
byte peak but also the cost composition of the buffers exposed to eviction.

Compiler back-ends have also long recognized the coupling between scheduling
and live ranges. Goodman--Hsu-style integrated prepass scheduling
~\citep{goodman1988integrated} and later register-pressure-aware schedulers
~\citep{shobaki2013preallocation,shobaki2022register} trade instruction-level
parallelism against register pressure. Local allocators and replacement
heuristics exploit the fact that clean values can be evicted without a store
~\citep{liberatore1999evaluation,guo2004belady}. Caching theory generalizes
Belady's farthest-future rule~\citep{belady1966study} to nonuniform-size and
nonuniform-cost objects~\citep{coester2022blockaware}. These results show that
``schedule affects liveness'' and ``clean victims are cheaper'' are not new in
isolation. The additional freedom in intra-kernel DAG scheduling is that the
request order itself can be changed: a compiler can actively shape live-range
overlap and clean/dirty composition rather than only choose victims for a fixed
stream.

Rematerialization and activation checkpointing reduce memory pressure from a
different direction~\citep{chen2016training,jain2019checkmate}; recent
spill-free compilation~\citep{chatarasi2026spillfree} aims to eliminate spills
by shortening live ranges. These techniques are complementary. In large
kernel-level NPU schedules, capacity constraints often make some spills
unavoidable. In that regime, reloadable input buffers form a natural clean
reserve, and the central question is how schedule order can reduce the write-back
cost of unavoidable evictions.
