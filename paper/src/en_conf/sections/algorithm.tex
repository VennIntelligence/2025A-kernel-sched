\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

We separate three roles with different evidence status. The \emph{production
solver} is a bounded candidate-order $\times$ online spill-planner portfolio and
the only default component. \emph{Cost-aware repair} is an offline case study of
whether cost semantics can improve an order. The \emph{fixed-order exact
planner} is a CP-SAT research oracle for lower bounds and certificates.

\subsection{Production Solver}\label{sec:scalable}

\paragraph{Dependency-frontier ordering.}
Many simultaneously ready allocations can let a one-input stream starve a
multi-input consumer and prolong its prepared operands. Dependency-frontier
schedules frees and executable operations first; when only allocations remain,
it completes the ready predecessor group of the earliest unlockable consumer
(Figure~\ref{fig:frontier}, Algorithm~\ref{alg:canonical}). The new frontier
order uses DAG structure and identifiers only. The portfolio also retains
capfit-id, the P1 order, and id-raw; we do not relabel the whole portfolio as
cost-aware or motif-agnostic. Complete tie rules are in Supplementary Sec.~3.1.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Canonical scalable solver}\label{alg:canonical}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require instance $I$, task $p\in\{1,2,3\}$
\If{$p=1$} \State \Return \Call{P1MemoryOrder}{$I$} \EndIf
\State $\mathcal O\gets\{\Call{Frontier}{I},\Call{CapFitID}{I}\}$
\State Add $\Call{P1Order}{I}$ and $\Call{IDRaw}{I}$ to $\mathcal O$
\State $\mathcal R\gets\{\textsc{DistCost},\textsc{Adaptive25}\}$
\State $\mathcal H\gets\{0,5,40\}$ if $p=2$, else $\{0,5,40,80,120\}$
\State $A^\star\gets\bot$; $k^\star\gets(+\infty,+\infty,+\infty)$
\ForAll{$(S,r,H)\in\mathcal O\times\mathcal R\times\mathcal H$}
  \State $A\gets\Call{BestFitPlan}{I,S,r,H}$
  \If{$A$ is feasible}
    \State $(\mathrm{Tr},n,T)\gets\Call{Evaluate}{A}$
    \State $k\gets(\mathrm{Tr},n,T)$ if $p=2$, else $(T,\mathrm{Tr},n)$
    \If{$k<k^\star$} \State $(A^\star,k^\star)\gets(A,k)$ \EndIf
  \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $A^\star$ \Comment{order, offsets, and spill entries}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Placement and eviction.}
Every base order uses true best-fit contiguous placement and compares a
distance/cost score with an adaptive fragmentation policy. Both are heuristics;
true-objective selection over complete artifacts absorbs local mistakes.
Scores, thresholds, relocation bounds, and tie rules are in Supplementary
Sec.~3.1 and Table~S2.

\paragraph{True-objective portfolio selection.}
P2/P3 form fixed portfolios over four orders, two policies, and three/five
reload windows, selecting complete artifacts by $(\mathrm{Tr},n,T)$ or
$(T,\mathrm{Tr},n)$. Infeasible members are skipped; no live-byte or overflow
surrogate selects the output. Window semantics and artifact generation are in
Supplementary Secs.~3.1--3.3.

\subsection{Cost-Aware Repair Case Studies}\label{sec:repair}

To probe order-level cost headroom, we try legal node or chain moves offline,
replan, and accept only strict P2-key improvements. Different
instances use different search procedures and budgets; small FlashAttention
probes find no gain and Matmul is not searched. Repair is therefore neither a
production stage nor a uniform algorithm. Budgets and negative probes appear in
Supplementary Secs.~3.4 and~4.5.

\subsection{Fixed-Order Weighted Residency Gaps}\label{sec:fixed-order}

Under a fixed order, consecutive mandatory events of each buffer define
residency gaps. Keeping a gap consumes capacity; interrupting it pays one
class-weighted charge. A binary model minimizes interruption cost under
per-cache, per-cut capacity. Its solution must still undergo contiguous packing,
spill-dependency generation, and canonical validation
(Figure~\ref{fig:gap-model}). Variables, cuts, the contiguity counterexample,
and model encoding are in Supplementary Secs.~2.2--2.3 and~3.4.

\begin{proposition}[Fixed-order traffic lower bound and certificate]
\label{prop:gap-certificate}
For a fixed order, the relaxation optimum lower-bounds every legal
contiguous-address spill plan. If an optimal gap selection packs contiguously
and its validated artifact has traffic equal to the bound, the artifact is
traffic-optimal for that fixed order under the benchmark model.
\end{proposition}

The proof maps any legal physical plan to a capacity-feasible gap selection.
The complete proof and a capacity-feasible but unpackable counterexample are in
Supplementary Secs.~2.2--2.3. The certificate optimizes fixed-order traffic only,
not spill count or time among ties, and proves no global order optimum.

\paragraph{Complexity and deployment.}
With fixed candidate counts, the scalable path is polynomial with a conservative
quadratic buffer term in placement; gap selection and fallback packing are
exponential in the worst case. CP-SAT remains a research oracle. Any integration
requires a threshold/timeout and validated fallback; details are in
Supplementary Sec.~2.5.
