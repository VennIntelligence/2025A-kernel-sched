\begin{abstract}
Deep-learning compilers lower neural operators into kernel-level DAGs whose
nodes mix micro-operations, short-lived tensors, and heterogeneous execution
pipes. Under tight on-chip cache capacity, two legal topological orders can have
similar peak pressure yet induce very different off-chip spill traffic. This
paper identifies a structural asymmetry that standard schedulers often miss:
clean buffers loaded from off-chip memory already have a backing copy and need not be
written back when evicted, whereas dirty buffers produced by computation must be
written before they are later reloaded. The two classes consume identical
on-chip capacity but differ by a factor of two in spill cost. We introduce
spill-cost-aware liveness shaping, which changes the legal schedule order to
control the clean/dirty composition exposed inside capacity-pressure windows.
The method keeps low-cost clean bytes available as eviction reserve and thereby
reduces the cost of unavoidable spills. We prove a one-sided certificate for
spill inevitability, give a linear-time overflow-area surrogate with a
conditional constant-factor connection to optimal spill traffic, and explain why
schedule order dominates victim-rule tuning under a Belady-margin condition.
Experiments on public NPU-style scheduling instances, synthetic DAG
distributions, small-graph oracles, and controlled clean/dirty ablations show
that clean/dirty-blind pressure schedulers can pay
$2.4\text{--}26\times$ more spill traffic in capacity-bound regimes.
\end{abstract}
